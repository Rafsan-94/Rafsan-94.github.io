The system I built to run my own job search

Mohammad Rafsan Hossain · Designed and run by me since late September 2026.

The problem

Applying for jobs well is slow. A tailored application takes hours of reading, scoring, writing, and checking. Applying fast is sloppy, and sloppy applications lose to both problems at once. I had the problem myself, so I built the tool I wished existed.

How it works

1. Sweep. Every morning the system scans job boards and company career pages for roles in the lanes I defined, and saves the full text of anything worth a closer look.

2. Score. Each posting is scored against rules I wrote. The core rule is a veto: if a role's main duties do not match my verified experience, the role dies, no matter how good the title looks. Fit, pay floor, and permanence are checked the same way.

3. Prepare. For roles that pass, I prepare a tailored application and check every requirement against my real experience, naming the gaps I don't have instead of covering them up.

4. Gate. The system cannot send anything. Every message, application, and submission stops and shows me the exact final content first. I approve it or it does not go. That one rule is why I trust it.

The failure log

The part I am proudest of was not planned. Early on, the system scored roles from short summaries instead of full posting text, and the scores came out inflated. Roles looked winnable that were not. The fix became a rule: no role is scored until its complete posting text is saved and read.

That became the pattern. Errors get written down, along with the change I made because of them. The log is long now. It is the reason the system gets more reliable every week instead of just busier.

The numbers so far

60+ applications in about five weeks, each with its own tailored resume and cover note, and each one checked and approved by me before it went out.

2 companies in real conversations so far, a final interview at one and a second call at the other. Not a huge hit rate. I would rather show that honestly than dress it up.

Daily operation since late September 2026: morning sorting and scoring, follow ups on open applications, and a weekly look at what the numbers actually say.

What is mine and what is the machine's

I wrote the rules, I set the thresholds, and I make every decision that leaves the system. The tools do the repetitive work: gathering, sorting, and tracking. When the system and I disagree, I read the evidence and one of us changes. That split is the product lesson I took from building it: people do not trust tools that ask for blind trust. They trust tools that show their work and stop at the right moments.
